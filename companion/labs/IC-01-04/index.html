Start with a physical question

Why does ignoring the detector result still modify a quantum state?

For a two-level system, the Born rule gives outcome probabilities directly from the overlap of state and analyzer. A quantum measurement is also a state transformation. When we select a particular result, the system is prepared in its associated eigenstate; when we sum over the unobserved outcomes, the resulting density operator generally becomes mixed. A bypassed apparatus instead applies the identity and leaves the preparation unchanged.

Predict before touching a control. Prepare a pure +z state. Rotate a measurement axis toward x. Predict P(+), and compare the state after recording + with the state after measuring but ignoring the result.

Prerequisites. Density operators ρ=(I+r·σ)/2, Bloch sphere and the orthogonal projector Born rule. Read these explanations at your own pace; the experiment is designed for private self-study alongside the lecture notes.

Backaction

How does an unrecorded measurement change purity?

An unselected rank-one projective measurement removes Bloch components transverse to the chosen axis.

After forgetting the outcome, r′=(r·n)n and Tr[(ρ′)²]=(1+(r·n)²)/2. A bypass preserves the original purity.

Derive the prediction

Probabilities and conditional density operators

Write the incoming qubit state ρ=(I+r·σ)/2 with |r|≤1. For measurement direction n, the two orthogonal rank-one projectors are P±=(I±n·σ)/2. The Born probability is a trace:

p± = Tr(ρP±) = [1±r·n]/2.

If result ± is observed, the Lüders rule prepares ρ±=P±ρP±/p±; this rank-one outcome has Bloch vector ±n whenever its probability is nonzero. If the result is ignored, the branches must be added as density operators, not state amplitudes:

ρ′=P+ρP++P−ρP−,   r′=(r·n)n.

A state with r transverse to n becomes mixed with shorter Bloch vector. Thus the physical act of projective readout and selective recording have separately describable effects. For an impossible selected event p±=0, a normalized conditional output state is undefined.

Check a limiting case directly

If r=0 the input is maximally mixed, both outcomes occur with probability 1/2, and nonselective readout leaves it unchanged. If the state starts in a measurement eigenstate, the corresponding certain outcome preserves its state. For r perpendicular to n, nonselective measurement produces I/2.

What assumptions define the calculation?

Ideal two-outcome qubit projective measurement, explicit result conditioning and Lüders nonselective update. General POVMs and multiple possible instruments sharing the same effects require additional structure and are developed in the companion's measurement-instrument laboratory.

Explore → compare → understand

Three independent investigations

  1. Compare selecting and forgetting. With |z+〉 input and x-axis measurement, select + and inspect the resulting pure x-state. Switch to ignored result and inspect purity 1/2. Bypass the device and compare.
  2. Check a certainty. Align n with r for a pure input. The + event is certain and the nonselective state remains unchanged. Rotate n through 180° and explain the swapped outcome probabilities.
  3. Mix the preparation. Decrease the initial Bloch length from 1 to 0. Inspect the flattening of Born probabilities and decide which experimental settings can still distinguish different prepared states.

Self-check: A pure |z+〉 qubit is measured in the x basis, with the result discarded. What is the state afterward?

Challenge: explicitly multiply P±ρP± using Pauli matrices to recover the dephasing identity for the Bloch vector.
