The physical question

When does total angular momentum remain a good quantum number, and how does a perturbation mix its multiplets?

Two spin-½ systems admit a four-dimensional tensor product, which can equally be reorganized into a spin-one triplet and a spin-zero singlet. Exchange symmetry and total spin become transparent in the coupled basis, while a magnetic-field gradient mixes the total-spin sectors. An exactly solvable two-state block makes this transition visible.

Before changing a parameter: At positive exchange J and zero gradient Δ, which is lower: the triplet or singlet? What happens when a strong opposite local field is applied to the spins?

Prerequisites: the corresponding lecture-note treatment of eigenstates, phases, normalization and elementary differential equations. Work through the calculation on paper before exploring parameter sweeps.

Recover the mathematics

Derivation and physical meaning

The two-spin Hilbert space decomposes as ½⊗½=1⊕0. In the product basis, the coupled triplet is |T+〉=|↑↑〉, |T₀〉=(|↑↓〉+|↓↑〉)/√2, and |T−〉=|↓↓〉; the singlet is |S〉=(|↑↓〉−|↓↑〉)/√2. The states are exactly orthonormal and resolve the identity.

S₁·S₂ = [S²−S₁²−S₂²]/2;   Etriplet=J/4, Esinglet=−3J/4.

Consider H=J S₁·S₂+Δ(S₁z−S₂z) in units ℏ=1. The gradient vanishes on |T±〉 but couples |T₀〉 and |S〉. In that ordered basis the relevant block is H₀=[[J/4,Δ],[Δ,−3J/4]], with exact energies E±=−J/4±√[(J/2)²+Δ²]. The gap √(J²+4Δ²) controls the mixing.

Within the m=0 subspace define σz=|T₀〉〈T₀|−|S〉〈S| and σx=|T₀〉〈S|+h.c. The eigenstate Bloch vector aligns or anti-aligns with (Δ,0,J/2). Its triplet probability P(T₀)=[1±(J/2)/√((J/2)²+Δ²)]/2. Total-spin expectation is 〈S²〉=2P(T₀), while spin-correlation expectation is 〈σ₁·σ₂〉=4P(T₀)−3. An external gradient breaks total-spin conservation while preserving total Sz=0.

Check an exact limiting case

At Δ=0 and J>0 the ground state is the singlet and the excited m=0 state is triplet; for J<0 their ordering reverses. At |Δ|≫|J| the eigenstates approach product spin configurations |↑↓〉 or |↓↑〉. At J=Δ=0 the m=0 block is exactly degenerate and its individual eigenvectors are not uniquely determined.

Scope and validity of the model

Two ideal spin-½ particles with isotropic Heisenberg exchange plus a static opposite local z field, using ℏ=1 and no spin-orbit anisotropy or decoherence. The model includes the full triplet spectrum but computes wavefunction weights in the m=0 sector; the degenerate origin is explicitly undefined.

Independent investigations

Explore and explain

  1. Verify Clebsch–Gordan structure. At Δ=0, identify the triplet and singlet projectors, confirm the exchange energy difference J and determine 〈S²〉 of each.
  2. Break total-spin symmetry. Increase |Δ| at fixed positive J. Explain why the energy levels avoid crossing and why the corresponding eigenstates approach product states.
  3. Recover a product measurement. Expand |T₀〉 and |S〉 back into |↑↓〉 and |↓↑〉. Compare the two product probabilities with the coupled-basis probabilities.

Self-check: For J>0 and Δ=0, which state has the lower exchange energy?

Synthesis and references

What the experiment establishes

The coupled basis is a physical classification by total spin. Its usefulness is decided by Hamiltonian symmetry: isotropic exchange respects S², while opposing fields mix S=0 and S=1. Students can compute both descriptions and confirm that probabilities and energies remain consistent.
