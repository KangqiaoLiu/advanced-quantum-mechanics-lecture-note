When do two paths interfere?

Start with a physical question

Why does detecting the path erase an interference fringe?

Consider two orthogonal routes |0〉 and |1〉 entering the final balanced recombination. Their complex probability amplitudes acquire a relative phase φ. The same total path populations may produce oscillating detector probabilities or a constant value, depending on whether the relative phase is preserved. By continuously suppressing off-diagonal coherence, we can isolate the physical effect of path information without changing the input intensities. The result is an instructive introduction to density matrices and the Born rule.

Predict before touching a control. For balanced paths (w=1/2), predict P(+) at φ=0 and φ=π, first with perfect coherence and then after complete dephasing. Which predictions depend on phase?

Prerequisites. Normalized complex vectors, inner products, and probabilities of orthogonal measurement outcomes. Read these explanations at your own pace; the experiment is designed for private self-study alongside the lecture notes.

Coherence versus contrast

What survives partial loss of phase information?

Varying coherence at a fixed path imbalance isolates the contribution of off-diagonal density-matrix elements.

The fringe visibility is V=2η√[w(1−w)]. This contrasts with path distinguishability D=|2w−1|; with ideal pure paths, V²+D²=1.

Derive the prediction

Interference from an off-diagonal density matrix

Start with |ψ〉=√w|0〉+eiφ√(1−w)|1〉. The final |+〉=(|0〉+|1〉)/√2 projection adds both complex amplitudes and then squares their magnitude:

P(+)=|〈+|ψ〉|²=½+√[w(1−w)]cosφ.

A phase-damping channel keeps the diagonal populations intact while multiplying the off-diagonal coherences by η. In the {|0〉,|1〉} basis, ρ₀₁=η√[w(1−w)]e−iφ. Since 〈+|ρ|+〉=½(ρ₀₀+ρ₁₁+ρ₀₁+ρ₁₀), we obtain

P(+)=½+η√[w(1−w)]cosφ,   P(−)=1−P(+).

The fringe visibility is the contrast (Pmax−Pmin)/(Pmax+Pmin), equal to 2η√[w(1−w)]. A global factor eiχ multiplying every amplitude cancels from every probability; only relative phase information appears in the interference term.

Check a limiting case directly

At w=0 or w=1, one path carries all the population. The interference term is zero even for η=1. At η=0, the detector is equally likely to register + or − for any phase, although the path populations can remain different. At w=1/2 and φ=0, perfect coherence yields P(+)=1.

What assumptions define the calculation?

Ideal balanced recombination and projective readout in the ± basis, with dephasing described phenomenologically by a single real retention factor 0≤η≤1. Beam splitters, detector imperfections and microscopic environment dynamics are omitted. Density-matrix purity quantifies statistical mixture, while visibility quantifies coherence in a specified basis.

Explore → compare → understand

Three independent investigations

  1. Build a fringe. Set w=1/2 and η=1, then sweep φ through a full revolution. Identify the constructive and destructive phases; explain why detector probabilities exchange while the total stays one.
  2. Remove only coherence. Keep w=.5 fixed and reduce η. Observe whether path populations change. Explain the monotonic decline in the fringe amplitude and the remaining value of P(+).
  3. Unbalance the two paths. Restore η=1 and move w toward 0.9. Compute the reduced visibility and compare with D=|2w−1|. Decide which information is acquired from the imbalance alone.

Self-check: With w=1/2, φ=π and η=0, what is P(+)?

Challenge: explicitly construct the two-by-two density matrix and calculate its eigenvalues and purity as η varies.

Interpretation and source links

What this experiment establishes

This model distinguishes a coherent superposition from an incoherent mixture with identical path populations. The operational signature is the relative-phase dependence of detector counts. The underlying principle will reappear in interferometry, quantum walks, Berry phases, and quantum circuits.
