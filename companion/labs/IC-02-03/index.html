Experiment 02 · Quantum time evolution

A moving wave packet in the harmonic oscillator

Watch position probability, phase-space structure and Fock occupations evolve together. Compare the coherent state's classical-looking orbit with a stationary number state and a rotating squeezed vacuum.

15–30 min guided experimentExact oscillator identitiesWigner phase spaceTime-dependent covariance

The physical question

Which quantum states move without changing their probability profile?

Individual energy eigenstates acquire only phase factors: their position probabilities are stationary. A coherent state superposes number states with precisely coordinated phases. Its wave-packet center follows the classical oscillator trajectory while the spatial variance stays constant. A squeezed vacuum makes the quantum fluctuations themselves evolve.

Initial prediction. Start with coherent |α〉 at α=1.5 and move time across one full oscillator period. What happens to the packet width? Then select number state |2〉. Is the position density still moving?

Mathematical derivation

Why does the coherent packet retain its shape?

With H = ℏω(a†a+½) and |α〉 satisfying a|α〉=α|α〉, write the initial state in the number basis:

|α〉 = e−|α|²/2 ∑n≥0 αn/√(n!) |n〉.

Each |n〉 gains a phase e−iωt(n+½). The resulting relative phases are exactly those of a new coherent state with α(t)=αe−iωt. Its center is a classical oscillator orbit.

〈x〉 = √2 Re(αe−iωt),   〈p〉 = √2 Im(αe−iωt),   Δx²=Δp²=½.
Derive the number-state position density

In oscillator units, ψn(x) = π−1/4Hn(x)e−x²/2/√(2nn!). Thus |ψn|² = e−x²Hn(x)²/(√π·2nn!). Hn obeys the recurrence Hn+1=2xHn−2nHn−1. Time changes only the global phase of each number state.

Why does the squeezed Gaussian rotate?

For ζ=reiφ, the initial covariance matrix has Vxx = ½(cosh2r−cosφ sinh2r), Vpp = ½(cosh2r+cosφ sinh2r), Vxp = −½sinφ sinh2r. Harmonic evolution applies a phase-space rotation R(t) to this matrix: V(t)=R(t)V(0)R(t)T. Its determinant remains ¼, while a single quadrature's variance oscillates.

Interpret the Wigner function

Gaussian states have positive Wigner functions W(z)=[2π√det V]−1exp[−½(z−μ)TV−1(z−μ)]. For |n〉, Wn(x,p)=(−1)nπ−1e−(x²+p²)Ln[2(x²+p²)], which can be negative. In all cases, integrating W over p yields the actual position density.

Independent exploration

Three informative comparisons

  1. Coherent oscillation. Set |α|=2, track the peak through quarter periods, then inspect 〈x〉 and the constant Δx². Which features agree with a classical phase-space orbit?
  2. Quantum nodes and negativity. Switch to |n=1〉 and |n=2〉. Locate spatial nodes and negative Wigner regions. These are differences between energy eigenstates and positive Gaussian states.
  3. Squeezing and its trade-off. Set squeeze r=1, vary the angle and time. Find where Δx² is minimal. Verify that the covariance determinant remains ¼ even when Δx²Δp² exceeds ¼ due to x–p covariance.

Self-check: For a coherent state in a harmonic potential, what happens to Δx² over one period?

Assumptions and further study

The plots describe an ideal one-dimensional harmonic oscillator with no environmental decoherence or anharmonicity. The coherent state is an infinite Fock superposition evaluated analytically; the occupation histogram truncates only the displayed bins and reports the omitted probability. Squeezed vacuum has strictly even number parity. For number states, the Wigner function is a signed quasiprobability, so it should never be interpreted as an ordinary joint probability over x and p.
