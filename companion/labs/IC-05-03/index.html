Lippmann–Schwinger and Born-series convergence

An exactly solvable one-dimensional delta potential reveals how the outgoing Green function repeatedly returns a wave to the same scatterer. Compare the exact unitary reflection amplitude against successive Born-series partial sums, and see why their radius of convergence matters.

Motivation and learning objective

How can the exact scattering amplitude stay finite while its Born series diverges?

An exactly solvable one-dimensional delta potential reveals how the outgoing Green function repeatedly returns a wave to the same scatterer. Compare the exact unitary reflection amplitude against successive Born-series partial sums, and see why their radius of convergence matters.

Predict before exploring. With k=1, g=1 the expansion parameter is |g/(2k)|=0.5. What happens to higher Born orders? What changes at g=4, where the expansion parameter is 2?

Recommended prerequisites: the relevant mathematical formulation in the English lecture notes, complex amplitudes, expectation values, and a basic understanding of quantum operators. Read the derivation below before interpreting the plots as physical observables.

Derivation and assumptions

Reconstruct the physical calculation

Set ℏ²/(2m)=1 and consider a one-dimensional scattering state with incident kinetic energy E=k² (k>0) and a short-range potential V(x)=gδ(x). The free outgoing resolvent is G₀⁺(x,x′;k)=−i exp[ik|x−x′|]/(2k), giving G₀⁺(0,0;k)=−i/(2k). This is the outgoing Green function specified by the radiation boundary condition.

T(E)=V+VG₀⁺T;   T₀₀=g/[1−gG₀⁺(0,0)] = g/(1+i g/2k).

The exact outgoing wave is ψ(x)=exp(ikx)+r exp(−ikx) for x<0 and ψ(x)=t exp(ikx) for x>0, where u=g/(2k), r=−iu/(1+iu), t=1/(1+iu). Reflection and transmission probabilities are R=u²/(1+u²), T=1/(1+u²), satisfying the exact current-conservation identity R+T=1.

Iterating Lippmann–Schwinger algebraically yields the Born series T₀₀,N=g Σj=0N−1(−iu)j, convergent only for |u|<1. The order-N reflected amplitude is rN=−iT₀₀,N/(2k), and the exact finite-order error is rN−rExact=−rExact(−iu)N, hence |rN−rExact|=|rExact||u|N.

A well-defined nonperturbative scattering amplitude may therefore coexist with a divergent perturbation series. The condition |u|<1 is the radius of convergence for this explicit geometric Born expansion, not a universal criterion for every scattering potential or three-dimensional partial-wave calculation. At negative g the model supports a bound state, but the displayed energy is positive and the on-shell scattering still satisfies unitarity.

Check exact and singular limits

At g→0, R≈g²/(4k²) and the first Born approximation becomes reliable. At |g|≫2k, exact R approaches one while the first Born probability grows without bound and higher geometric partial sums diverge. At g=0, r=0 and t=1. At |u|=1, the series lies on its convergence boundary and the exact amplitude is nevertheless finite.

Numerical and physical validity conditions

Ideal one-dimensional delta potential, continuous line, positive incident energy and an outgoing Green-function boundary condition. The delta interaction is used to supply an analytically exact multiple-scattering benchmark. No three-dimensional angular differential cross section is inferred from this one-dimensional model.

Active understanding

Three independent investigations

  1. Recover the exact solution. Integrate the Schrödinger equation across x=0 to obtain the derivative jump ψ′(0+)−ψ′(0−)=gψ(0), then rederive r and t.
  2. Test the Born convergence circle. Increase |u| through unity and add terms. Separate the behavior of the exact amplitude from that of its series.
  3. Distinguish amplitude from probability. Square the truncated rN and compare against the exact R. Explain why a finite Born approximation need not satisfy exact scattering unitarity.

Self-check: For the delta scatterer, what condition guarantees convergence of the geometric Born T-series?

Extension: reproduce one model with a separate implementation or an AI-assisted notebook. Check numerical convergence, normalizations, phase conventions, or independent limiting-case results before trusting the answer.

Physical conclusions

Interpret the invariant or approximation carefully

This model links the outgoing Green function, T-matrix resummation, perturbative convergence and unitary scattering in one independently solvable experiment. It explicitly demonstrates why a strong-scattering calculation may require resummation even though each perturbative step is algebraically simple.
