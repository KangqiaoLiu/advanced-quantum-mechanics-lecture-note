Derive and compare

Mathematical model

Consider the singlet |Ψ−〉=(|↑↓〉−|↓↑〉)/√2. Spin measurements along axes a and b return random values A,B∈{−1,+1}, with correlator E(a,b)=〈(a·σ)⊗(b·σ)〉=−a·b. If the axes lie in a common plane and angles are measured in degrees, E(a,b)=−cos(a−b). The Werner mixture ρ=v|Ψ−〉〈Ψ−|+(1−v)I₄/4 multiplies this correlator by visibility v.

E(a,b)=−v cos(a−b);   p(s,t|a,b)=[1−st v cos(a−b)]/4.

Combine four independently chosen settings into S=E(a,b)+E(a,b′)+E(a′,b)−E(a′,b′). A local-hidden-variable theory assigning outcomes A(a,λ), A(a′,λ), B(b,λ), B(b′,λ) to each λ gives an expression A(B+B′)+A′(B−B′), whose magnitude equals 2 for every deterministic λ. Averaging any distribution of such λ therefore yields |S|≤2.

Quantum mechanics permits |S|≤2√2: the Tsirelson bound. For a=0°, a′=90°, b=45°, b′=−45°, the singlet predicts S=−2√2v. Thus this ideal geometry violates the local bound exactly when v>1/√2. This threshold refers to the Werner family, ideal projective detection, and perfect independent preparation; real experimental loophole analysis requires further controls.

The joint-outcome chart allows a second route to calculating E=P++ +P−−−P+−−P−+. Equal marginal probabilities alone never imply classical correlations: the nonlocal signature is the inconsistency of all four conditional correlations with a shared local model.

Test an exactly soluble physical limit

For v=0 all E and S vanish and all four joint outcomes are equally likely. At v=1 with a=b, measurement results are perfectly anticorrelated. At the symmetric quantum-optimal angles |S|=2√2. At precisely v=1/√2 the ideal maximum reaches the local bound but does not exceed it.

Assumptions, uncertainties, and reliability

Ideal spatially separated qubit singlet/Werner mixtures, coplanar spin axes, binary projective outcomes, fair sampling, and no detection or communication loopholes. Correlation plots are theoretical expectations, not a loophole-free experimental Bell test.

Explore independently

Three worked investigations

  1. Derive a bound. Write the deterministic local expression A(B+B′)+A′(B−B′) and explain why at least one parenthesis is zero, giving |S|=2.
  2. Find the quantum optimum. Fix the 0°,90° Alice axes and sweep symmetric Bob settings. Identify β≈45° and compare the reported S with 2√2.
  3. Add noise. Decrease v with optimal geometry. Locate the threshold 1/√2; then reconstruct the correlator from the four actual joint probabilities.

Self-check: What is the maximal CHSH magnitude predicted by quantum theory for two dichotomic observables per party?

Extended exercise: derive one prediction independently, identify the model approximation or physical symmetry that supports it, and test its numerical convergence. AI guidance is useful for generating further test cases after the derivation has been checked.

Interpretation

What survives beyond the model

Quantum correlations depend on the joint choice of incompatible observables, and four complementary spin settings reveal an experimentally meaningful difference from local hidden-variable predictions. The model separates local marginals, bipartite probabilities, noise sensitivity and the global CHSH constraint.
