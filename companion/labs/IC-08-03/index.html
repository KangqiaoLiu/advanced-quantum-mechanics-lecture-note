When does an SSH boundary support a localized state?

Change the termination of the very same alternating-bond system, inspect exact finite-chain eigenvectors, and explore what happens when chiral symmetry is broken.

Self-paced guided physicsBulk–boundary matchingEdge-level hybridizationReproducible numerical checks

Physical question

Does the bulk winding guarantee an edge mode for every possible cut?

A bulk one-dimensional winding number is defined only after specifying its Bloch basis, unit cell and protecting symmetry. Edge states require a compatible physical termination: which bonds are cut matters. A finite chain also contains two ends, and modes localized near opposite boundaries may overlap and split away from exact zero. The presence or absence of a near-zero mode, and its robustness under a staggered potential, provide a concrete route to understanding both the power and the limits of bulk–boundary correspondence.

Make a prediction. Choose t₂≫t₁ with Δ=0. For a chain cut across the intercell bonds, predict which ends are weakly connected and whether near-zero states appear. Shift the cut by one site and explain why the outcome changes.

Prerequisites: The SSH Bloch Hamiltonian, alternating hopping terms, sublattice/chiral symmetry, and finite-dimensional matrix diagonalization.

Mathematical reconstruction

Termination and symmetry are part of bulk–boundary correspondence

With sublattice orbitals Aⱼ and Bⱼ, the original real-space SSH Hamiltonian has alternating hoppings t₁A†ⱼBⱼ and t₂A†ⱼ₊₁Bⱼ, plus Hermitian conjugates. An open chain containing complete cells A₁,B₁,…,Aₙ,Bₙ ends with intracell t₁ bonds. The alternative cut shifts the 2N-site chain by one lattice site, so its end bonds are t₂ while its interior alternation is reversed.

HSSH=Σⱼ[t₁A†ⱼBⱼ+t₂A†ⱼ₊₁Bⱼ+h.c.],   ΓHΓ=−H (Δ=0).

For the weak-end termination and t₂>t₁, the semi-infinite zero-energy recurrence on the A sublattice gives t₁Aⱼ+t₂Aⱼ₊₁=0. Hence successive edge amplitudes are multiplied by −t₁/t₂ and the localization length (in cells) is ξ=1/ln(t₂/t₁). The edge state is normalizable if |t₁/t₂|<1.

On a finite chain two boundaries support overlapping exponentially localized solutions. Exact eigenenergies generally split into ±ε rather than remaining exactly zero. Switching the termination changes which weak and strong links are cut, while the original bulk Bloch convention stays fixed. Adding ±Δ onsite energies on A and B breaks the chiral anticommutation: its edge-related energies can move away from zero without forcing a bulk-band transition.

Exactly checkable limits

For t₁=0 and t₂>0, the intercell-cut chain has two exactly disconnected end sites and two exact zero modes; the shifted termination forms N disconnected t₂ dimers and has no zero mode. The UI uses t₁≥0.1, but a numerical limit approaches this behavior. At t₁=t₂, the bulk gap closes and a winding number ceases to be defined.

Assumptions and error sources

Noninteracting single-particle SSH tight-binding chain with two alternating positive real hoppings, two open-boundary terminations and possible sublattice-staggered onsite energy. The term 'topological' references the fixed original unit cell and Δ=0; the shifted cut and symmetry breaking modify which boundary statements apply.

Investigate independently

Follow three scientific questions

  1. Hold the bulk, change the cut. Use t₁=.5,t₂=1.5 and N=10. Compare the exact spectrum and closest-to-zero eigenvector for two endings. Explain which termination supports weakly paired boundary orbitals.
  2. Explore finite-size splitting. Choose the intercell cut, Δ=0 and t₂>t₁. Change N and inspect the middle levels. Relate the shrinking overlap to powers of |t₁/t₂|.
  3. Remove symmetry protection. Add a staggered Δ. Check how the midgap states respond and why the two-band gap need not close when exact zero-energy pinning is lost.

Self-check: For t₂>t₁>0 and Δ=0, which termination has exponentially localized SSH end modes?

Extension: compare eigenstate localization as t₁/t₂ approaches unity, and distinguish edge-state hybridization from its disappearance at the bulk gap closure.

Interpretation and further reading

What the result teaches

The physically relevant boundary must match the bulk Bloch and unit-cell convention. Topological winding predicts robust features under its stated symmetry and termination assumptions; finite-size hybridization and staggered onsite terms demonstrate what the integer alone cannot determine.
